Section 03 • ECEC Pedagogical Implementation

Two Sustainable Activity Plans

Play-based, intentional learning experiences aligned with EYLF V2.0 outcomes for children aged 4–5 years.

Based on My Two Blankets (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014)

Activity 1: Weaving Our Words — Crafting Collaborative Friendship Blankets

Preschool / Kindergarten (4–5 Years)

Learning Focus / Intentional Teaching

Intentional teaching focuses on fostering cultural empathy, language diversity and community belonging as fundamental dimensions of social sustainability (Australian Government Department of Education [AGDE], 2022). Educators guide children to explore how words, non-verbal gestures and welcoming actions contribute to inclusive learning environments (Elliott et al., 2024).

Fostering the Four Capabilities

  • Agency: Children select colours, fabric textures and multilingual greeting symbols to contribute to a communal classroom loom (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014).
  • Curiosity: Children explore diverse home languages and non-verbal expressions of friendship represented across the group (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).
  • Creativity: Children experiment with textile manipulation, ribbon weaving and visual welcome-card design.
  • Critical Thinking: Children reflect on the story's cold waterfall and warm blanket metaphors, identifying practical ways to help new peers feel safe and valued (Boldermo & Ødegaard, 2019).

Required Resources & Visual Documentation

  • Copy of My Two Blankets by Irena Kobald and Freya Blackwood
  • Cardboard weaving looms and communal classroom loom frame
  • Varied yarn, recycled fabric strips, felt scraps and ribbon
  • Multilingual greeting cards created with families
  • Photo display board documenting peer collaboration
Visual Setup Provocation: Arrange textured fabrics in cool blues to represent the waterfall alongside warm terracotta, gold and ochre threads to represent the friendship blanket. Include family home-language greeting cards.

Implementation Process / Steps

Phase 1

Introductory Phase (Shared Reading & Provocation)

The educator gathers children on the reflection rug and reads My Two Blankets (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014). Open-ended questions encourage children to explore the story's sustainability themes: "How did Cartwheel feel when she heard the cold waterfall of words? What made her old blanket feel warm? How can we help new friends feel welcome in our classroom?" (Elliott et al., 2024).

Phase 2

Main Activity (Collaborative Weaving Exploration)

Children move to the main activity area for hands-on, play-based exploration (AGDE, 2022). Working in small groups, they weave fabric strips, yarn and multilingual greeting cards into a large communal Friendship Blanket. Educators facilitate sustained shared thinking, scaffold peer communication and encourage children to explain how each contribution strengthens collective belonging (Weldemariam et al., 2022).

Phase 3

Closing Phase (Circle Reflection)

Children gather on the reflection rug around their completed Friendship Blanket. The educator asks: "How will our blanket help someone who feels lonely or new in our room?" Children discuss how their shared creation represents safety, friendship and ongoing social support (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).

EYLF V2.0 Learning Outcomes

Outcome 1: Children have a strong sense of identity

1.1 Children feel safe, secure and supported. 1.4 Children learn to interact with others with care, empathy and respect (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 2: Children are connected with and contribute to their world

2.2 Children respond to diversity with respect. 2.4 Children become socially responsible and show respect for the environment (AGDE, 2022).

Based on The Invisible Boy (Ludwig & Barton, 2013)

Activity 2: Making the Invisible Visible — Collaborative Storymapping & Inclusion Cards

Preschool / Foundation (4–5 Years)

Learning Focus / Intentional Teaching

Intentional teaching centres on developing relational equity, active peer empathy and collaborative problem-solving within early learning communities (Hawkins, 2014). Educators scaffold children's ability to recognise peers who may be overlooked or excluded and develop practical strategies for social inclusion (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).

Fostering the Four Capabilities

  • Agency: Children take the initiative to invite peers into group tasks and create personal encouragement messages (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).
  • Curiosity: Children investigate how simple gestures and appreciation of diverse identities, including cultural foods such as bulgogi, build peer relationships (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).
  • Creativity: Children co-author comic-strip story panels and create colourful illustrations showing social inclusion.
  • Critical Thinking: Children examine scenarios involving playground exclusion and formulate practical solutions that promote fairness (Hawkins, 2014).

Required Resources & Visual Documentation

  • Copy of The Invisible Boy by Trudy Ludwig and Patrice Barton
  • Three-panel drawing paper, watercolour paints and coloured markers
  • Sticky-note pads for peer appreciation messages
  • Scenario cards depicting everyday playground situations
  • Photo exhibition board displaying children's collaborative story panels
Visual Setup Provocation: Pair charcoal and graphite pencils, representing grayscale isolation, with bright watercolour palettes so children can illustrate the transition towards inclusion.

Implementation Process / Steps

Phase 1

Introductory Phase (Visual Provocation & Dialogue)

The educator introduces The Invisible Boy during group time, highlighting Brian's emotional transition from grayscale to full colour (Ludwig & Barton, 2013). Open-ended questions encourage reflection: "Why did Brian feel invisible at kickball? How did Brian's note make Justin feel after others laughed at his bulgogi? What can we do when someone near us feels left out?" (Hawkins, 2014).

Phase 2

Main Activity (Three-Panel Collaborative Storymapping)

Children form small project groups of two or three and extend the story themes through collaborative learning (AGDE, 2022). Each group selects a scenario card illustrating a peer challenge, such as choosing teams or lunch seating. Children create a three-panel story map showing the problem, a caring action and an inclusive resolution. Educators model inclusive language and facilitate sustained shared thinking throughout the activity (Elliott et al., 2024).

Phase 3

Closing Phase (Notice & Welcome Board Launch)

Children gather in a reflection circle and present their group story maps. The educator establishes a permanent Notice and Welcome Board where children can leave drawings and appreciation messages for one another. The board provides an ongoing opportunity to practise inclusion and social responsibility (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).

EYLF V2.0 Learning Outcomes

Outcome 1: Children have a strong sense of identity

1.4 Children learn to interact with others with care, empathy and respect (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 2: Children are connected with and contribute to their world

2.1 Children develop a sense of belonging to groups and communities and an understanding of reciprocal rights and responsibilities (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 5: Children are effective communicators

5.1 Children interact verbally and non-verbally with others for a range of purposes (AGDE, 2022).
