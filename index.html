Introduction: Importance of Sustainability Picture Books

According to the Australian Government Department of Education [AGDE] (2022), sustainability education in early childhood must encompass holistic social, economic, and ecological dimensions, positioning young children as active, rights-bearing participants in building a fair and equitable world. Early Childhood Education for Sustainability (ECEfS) extends beyond traditional environmental conservation to address critical issues of social justice, diversity, equity, inclusion, and community responsibility. As Elliott et al. (2024) argue, embedding sociopolitical sustainability within early childhood settings enables children to critically examine everyday social dynamics and cultivate empathy across diverse communities.

Picture books serve as a powerful pedagogical medium in ECEfS by translating complex, abstract concepts of social equity into tangible, relational narratives. Sneddon and Pettit (2024) highlight that engaging young learners through narrative inquiry fosters children's active agency, curiosity, creativity, and critical thinking as they navigate story-based dilemmas. Through story reflection, children are encouraged to question social exclusion, imagine empathetic solutions, and enact positive change within their immediate early learning environments.

This pedagogical approach directly aligns with the Early Years Learning Framework V2.0 (AGDE, 2022), specifically the Sustainability Principle, which mandates that early childhood educators foster children's understanding of rights, fairness, and social responsibility. Hawkins (2014) notes that teaching for social justice through literature provides a foundation for relational pedagogy, where educators and children co-construct learning environments grounded in mutual respect. By grounding social sustainability in story sharing, early childhood settings cultivate inclusive communities where every child feels visible, valued, and connected.

Four Pedagogical Pillars

  • Agency: Enacting positive social change in peer spaces.
  • Curiosity: Exploring diverse languages, foods, and lived stories.
  • Creativity: Expressing empathy through weaving and visual storymapping.
  • Critical Thinking: Questioning exclusion and unfairness in everyday play.
