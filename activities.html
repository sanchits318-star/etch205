Section 03 • ECEC Pedagogical Implementation

Two Sustainable Activity Plans

Play-based, intentional learning experiences aligned with EYLF V2.0 outcomes for children aged 4–5 years.

Based on My Two Blankets (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014)

Activity 1: Weaving Our Words — Crafting Collaborative Friendship Blankets

Preschool / Kindergarten (4–5 Years)

Learning Focus / Intentional Teaching

In this activity, intentional teaching focuses on fostering cultural empathy, language diversity, and community belonging as fundamental pillars of social sustainability (AGDE, 2022). Educators intentionally guide children to analyze how words, non-verbal gestures, and welcoming actions create inclusive learning spaces (Elliott et al., 2024).

Fostering the Four Capabilities

  • Agency: Promoted as children select colors, fabric textures, and multilingual greeting symbols to weave into a communal classroom loom (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014).
  • Curiosity: Sparked when children explore diverse home languages and non-verbal signals of friendship represented across the group (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).
  • Creativity: Nurtured through textile manipulation, ribbon weaving, and designing visual welcome cards (Kobald & Blackwood, 2014).
  • Critical Thinking: Challenged as children reflect on the story metaphor of the cold waterfall versus the warm blanket, evaluating concrete actions to help new peers feel safe and valued (Boldermo & Odegaard, 2019).

Required Resources & Visual Documentation

  • Copy of My Two Blankets by Irena Kobald and Freya Blackwood
  • Cardboard weaving looms & communal classroom loom frame
  • Varied yarn, recycled fabric strips, felt scraps, and ribbon
  • Multilingual greeting cards created with families
  • Photo display board documenting peer collaboration
Visual Setup Provocation: Arrange textured fabrics in cool blues ("waterfall") alongside warm terracotta, gold, and ochre threads ("friendship blanket") with family home-language greeting cards.

Implementation Process / Steps

Phase 1

Introductory Phase (Shared Reading & Provocation)

The educator gathers children on the reflection rug and reads My Two Blankets by Kobald and Blackwood (2014). The educator embeds intentional teaching and open-ended questioning to explore the book's key sustainability concepts, asking: "How did Cartwheel feel when she heard the cold waterfall of words? What made her old blanket feel warm? How can we help new friends feel welcome in our classroom?" (Elliott et al., 2024).

Phase 2

Main Activity (Collaborative Weaving Exploration)

Children transition from the shared reading to the main activity area for hands-on, play-based exploration (AGDE, 2022). Working in small collaborative groups, children weave fabric strips, yarn, and multilingual greeting cards into a large communal Friendship Blanket loom. Educators engage in sustained shared thinking, scaffolding peer communication and encouraging children to explain how each added thread strengthens the group's collective belonging (Weldemariam et al., 2022).

Phase 3

Closing Phase (Circle Reflection)

Children gather back on the rug wrapped around their completed Friendship Blanket for a reflective group discussion (AGDE, 2022). The educator facilitates reflection by asking: "How will our blanket help someone who feels lonely or new in our room?" Children discuss how their woven creation represents shared safety and ongoing social support (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).

EYLF V2.0 Learning Outcomes

Outcome 1: Children have a strong sense of identity

1.1 Children feel safe, secure and supported; 1.4 Children learn to interact in relation to others with care, empathy and respect (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 2: Children are connected with and contribute to their world

2.2 Children respond to diversity with respect; 2.4 Children become socially responsible and show respect for the environment (AGDE, 2022).

Based on The Invisible Boy (Ludwig & Barton, 2013)

Activity 2: Making the Invisible Visible — Collaborative Storymapping & Inclusion Cards

Preschool / Foundation (4–5 Years)

Learning Focus / Intentional Teaching

Intentional teaching centers on developing relational equity, active peer empathy, and collaborative problem-solving within early learning communities (Brown, 2017). Educators intentionally scaffold children's ability to recognize quiet or excluded peers and co-create proactive strategies for social inclusion (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).

Fostering the Four Capabilities

  • Agency: Fostered as children take the initiative to invite peers into group tasks and author personal encouragement notes (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).
  • Curiosity: Engaged as children investigate how simple gestures and appreciation of diverse identities, such as sharing cultural foods like bulgogi, build deep peer relationships (Magiropoulos & Giugni, 2007).
  • Creativity: Expressed as children co-author comic-strip story panels and create vibrant watercolor illustrations showing social inclusion (Ludwig & Barton, 2013).
  • Critical Thinking: Activated as children analyze scenario cards depicting playground exclusion and formulate actionable solutions to ensure equity (Hawkins, 2014).

Required Resources & Visual Documentation

  • Copy of The Invisible Boy by Trudy Ludwig and Patrice Barton
  • Three-panel drawing paper, watercolor paints, and colored markers
  • Sticky note pads for peer appreciation messages
  • Scenario cards depicting everyday playground moments
  • Photo exhibition board of children's collaborative story panels
Visual Setup Provocation: Pair charcoal/graphite pencils (representing grayscale isolation) with bright watercolor palettes so children visually illustrate the shift to radiant inclusion.

Implementation Process / Steps

Phase 1

Introductory Phase (Visual Provocation & Dialogue)

The educator introduces The Invisible Boy during group time, using provocations to highlight Brian's emotional transition from grayscale to full color (Ludwig & Barton, 2013). The educator models responsive interaction and asks open-ended questions: "Why did Brian feel invisible at kickball? How did Brian's note make Justin feel after others laughed at his bulgogi? What can we do when someone near us feels left out?" (Hawkins, 2014).

Phase 2

Main Activity (Three-Panel Collaborative Storymapping)

Children transition to small project groups of two or three children, extending story themes through hands-on learning (AGDE, 2022). Each group selects a scenario card illustrating an everyday peer challenge (e.g., choosing teams or lunch seating) and collaborates to draw a three-panel story map showing the problem, a caring action, and an inclusive resolution. Educators provide scaffolding, model inclusive language, and facilitate sustained shared thinking during the creative process (Elliott et al., 2024).

Phase 3

Closing Phase (Notice & Welcome Board Launch)

The class gathers in a reflection circle where children present their group story maps to peers (AGDE, 2022). The educator establishes a permanent Notice and Welcome Board in the classroom, where children can leave sticky-note drawings and appreciation messages for one another, revisiting the key social sustainability concepts explored in the activity (Sneddon & Pettit, 2024).

EYLF V2.0 Learning Outcomes

Outcome 1: Children have a strong sense of identity

1.4 Children learn to interact in relation to others with care, empathy and respect (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 2: Connected with & contribute to their world

2.1 Children develop a sense of belonging to groups and communities and an understanding of reciprocal rights and responsibilities (AGDE, 2022).

Outcome 5: Children are effective communicators

5.1 Children interact verbally and non-verbally with others for a range of purposes (AGDE, 2022).
