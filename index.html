Section 01

Introduction: Importance of Sustainability Picture Books

According to the Australian Government Department of Education (AGDE, 2022), sustainability education in early childhood must encompass holistic social, economic and ecological dimensions, positioning young children as active participants in building a fair and equitable world. Early Childhood Education for Sustainability (ECEfS) extends beyond traditional environmental conservation to address social justice, diversity, equity, inclusion and community responsibility. Elliott et al. (2024) explain that embedding sociopolitical sustainability within early childhood settings enables children to examine everyday social dynamics and develop empathy across diverse communities.

Picture books provide a powerful pedagogical medium in ECEfS by translating complex concepts of social equity into meaningful narratives. Sneddon and Pettit (2024) highlight the importance of integrating social sustainability into everyday early childhood practices. Narrative inquiry can encourage children's agency, curiosity, creativity and critical thinking as they explore story-based situations. Through story reflection, young learners can question social exclusion, imagine empathetic solutions and contribute to positive change within their learning environments.

This pedagogical approach aligns with the Early Years Learning Framework V2.0 (AGDE, 2022), particularly its Sustainability Principle, which encourages educators to foster children's understanding of rights, fairness and social responsibility. Hawkins (2014) explains the importance of early childhood education in developing positive attitudes towards difference, diversity, equity and justice. By grounding social sustainability in shared reading and discussion, early childhood settings can cultivate inclusive communities where every child feels visible, valued and connected.

Four Pedagogical Pillars

  • Agency: Enacting positive social change in peer spaces.
  • Curiosity: Exploring diverse languages, foods and lived experiences.
  • Creativity: Expressing empathy through weaving and visual storymapping.
  • Critical Thinking: Questioning exclusion and unfairness in everyday play.
